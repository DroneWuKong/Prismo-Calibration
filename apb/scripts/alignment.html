ASSISTED MANUAL BORESIGHT & LEVER ARMS

Bring your cameras into line.

Watch the views together while you adjust the physical mounts. Use a distant, stationary feature for rough boresight, then measure each sensor’s position on the rig.

Bench procedure and limits — read before adjusting mounts
  1. Remove propellers and keep aircraft disarmed. Fix focus, zoom and image format. Choose one reference camera and a far, high-contrast target visible in every view.
  2. Use crosshairs and a long horizontal/vertical feature to adjust pointing and roll. Click the common target in each view to show its pixel offset. Different lenses, field of view and sensor spacing cause differences; try a second target distance to expose parallax.
  3. Tighten the mounts and recheck. Record the sensing points, one physical origin and positive axes for lever arms. Record an uncertainty estimate or leave it unknown.
  4. Freeze a final observation and export the worksheet. After moving a mount, collect new numerical rig-calibration recordings if you need calibrated transforms.

The image center is an approximate aiming guide. An optional matching lens result places the crosshair at its principal point. Views stay unwarped; comparison zoom is display-only. Visual agreement does not measure a rigid rotation, synchronized exposure or physical accuracy.

Connect two browser-visible cameras, or load one JPEG/PNG per view. Up to eight views; camera availability depends on the browser, driver and USB bandwidth.

MANUAL TRANSLATION MEASUREMENTS

Measure the lever arms.

Use one marked rig origin for every position. Measure to each sensing point: describe the camera center estimate, IMU chip location, flow sensor or GNSS antenna point you actually used. Keep case/board estimates explicit.

Enter positions in that one frame. Changing axis descriptions redefines the coordinates; check every measurement again. Unit changes convert the entered values. Leave unmeasured positions empty.
